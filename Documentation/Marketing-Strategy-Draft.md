# 9th Kingdom — six-week promotion and supporter strategy

Draft for John, 10 September 2026. Recommendations, not approved commitments. No site changes, publishing, outreach, spending or payment changes have been performed.

## Objective and assumption

### John's narrative clarification — 10 September 2026

The survival game opens the larger integrity journey; these are connected stages of one story. Lead public promotion with Chapter 1: Edge of Extinction and the scientist isolated in a biosphere as a human catastrophe unfolds. Explain the long-term vision as rebuilding society through eight journeys of challenge, repeated practice and service, guided by CIDCORE, the keeper of knowledge. The goal is becoming a 9th King: a servant of society whose worth is demonstrated through the challenges.

Proposed public hook: **“Five years studying a living world. Ten years to survive the world outside.”** Follow with a precise explanation that ten years is the outside robots' prediction. This is draft copy, not approved publication text.

Chapter 2: **Others** introduces contact with other survivors. Keep that revelation out of an initial Chapter 1 trailer unless John approves spoilers. The eight later journeys are a long-term vision, not promised launch content. This clarification resolves the narrative positioning question below; the existing public funding allocation still needs explicit review before changing donation messaging. Do not imply that contributing money earns the title of 9th King or proves integrity.

Build an audience that understands Chapter 1: Edge of Extinction, follows tangible development, and voluntarily helps fund the next achievable milestone. Assume a small existing audience and limited marketing budget; actual traffic, supporter totals and available budget are unknown. Begin with organic promotion and measure a baseline before setting revenue expectations.

## Resolve the public story first

The current homepage presents an integrity-based online experience with treasure journeys, tokens and a reward treasury. The support page describes donations primarily building that treasury and allocates 20% to CIDCORE infrastructure. This differs from the Unreal survival game now in development and from a request to fund more development assistance.

Recommendation: give the game a clear landing page, for example `/game`, with a distinct game-development support route and an explanation of its relationship to the wider 9th Kingdom. If the game becomes the main public identity, revise the homepage deliberately after John approves that decision. Existing treasury contributions must not silently become game-development money. Review existing promises and actual fund accounting before introducing revised fundraising copy.

Proposed positioning: “One human sanctuary. A living world beyond the glass. Explore through robots in 9th Kingdom: Edge of Extinction.”

Follow immediately with “An independent survival game in development” and a short explanation of John as its creator. Use actual prototype footage. Label concepts and future systems clearly. The five-dome habitat, robot control and lift can demonstrate real work; mysteries, expanded observation systems and future locations must be described as planned unless implemented and verified.

## Audience and message

1. Survival and exploration players: sanctuary, remote exploration, environmental storytelling and future mysteries.
2. People interested in realistic environments and architectural games: the ornate conservatory, white/brass materials, valley and countryside.
3. Blender and Unreal creators: making an editable asset, finding and fixing problems, and turning it into a playable environment.

Lead with the experience and John's personal commitment. Explain AI-assisted development as part of the process where relevant. Supporters should understand the work their contribution advances, rather than receiving a vague request for more AI usage.

## Landing page and supporter experience

Suggested order:

- A strong in-engine image and a 20–40 second gameplay/progress clip.
- A two-sentence description, current development status and platform plans only where decided.
- Primary action: “Follow development”; secondary action: “Support the next milestone”.
- A compact “Working now / Next / Later” roadmap.
- A concrete milestone with cost assumptions, progress evidence and any uncertain dependencies.
- A short note from John and dated updates.
- FAQ: what contributions fund, whether they include a game copy (do not imply one), current availability, and where to ask questions.

Provide voluntary email signup with clear consent and unsubscribe. A free follower should remain welcome. Do not require a donation to see ordinary progress. After support, show a thank-you and the next update date; let supporters choose whether their names appear publicly.

Do not claim charitable status, tax deductibility, guaranteed delivery or financial returns. Keep development support distinct from treasure eligibility and any future asset purchases. Specific payment, accounting and legal arrangements need review against the actual setup before launch.

## Fund one visible milestone

Proposed first milestone: a short, presentable journey from the conservatory to a richer valley outlook, with repaired architecture, a credible foreground terrace/steps, and the beginning of the mountain backdrop. Preserve sealed-habitat requirements when designing exterior steps. Scope to what can be finished and shown; do not promise the entire open world in six weeks.

Before naming a fundraising target, list:

| Cost | How to establish it |
| --- | --- |
| Development tools and AI assistance | Actual subscription/usage budget for the milestone |
| Assets | Audit owned/free content, then price specific remaining gaps |
| Hosting and services | Actual incremental cost and billing period |
| Payment fees and contingency | Provider terms and an explicit reserve |
| John's paid development time, if included | State it openly and use a defined amount |

Publish the target and allocation only after John verifies them. Use actual receipts for any progress bar; distinguish pledged, received, fees and spending. Report unused funds and changes honestly. Start with the existing payment provider where suitable; do not create extra memberships and channels without a clear need.

Suggested contribution choices to test: $5, $15, $30 and a custom amount, in the payment route's supported currency. These are suggestions, not forecasts. Add recurring support only if John wants it and the provider supports clear cancellation. Keep benefits low-maintenance: optional credits and an existing monthly development recap. Avoid bespoke assets, lifetime access and promises that consume development time.

## Content people have a reason to share

Use three recurring themes: the sanctuary being built, the world beyond it, and seeing through a robot's eyes. A practical weekly batch is three 15–40 second clips, one short development recap and a few replies to genuine questions.

First six clip ideas:

1. Blender assembly to playable five-dome habitat: show both honestly.
2. “Why did the pillar bases disappear?” Show the inverted-face repair and finished white/brass result.
3. A robot-eye walk, using only the working control system.
4. Current terrain versus a first rustic foreground improvement once built.
5. Montana-inspired versus Alpine reference direction: invite preference without pretending concepts are game footage.
6. John's short explanation of why he is making this world.

Open with the most interesting image or question. Give one concrete piece of progress, then one gentle call to action. Add captions and a readable website address. Use in-engine footage as the default proof; concepts need an explicit label.

Proposed channel mix: YouTube for a lasting home for walkthroughs and short clips, plus one short-form/social channel John already enjoys using. Select the second channel after checking existing audience data. Participate selectively in relevant communities, observing their promotion rules. Do not post identical donation requests across groups or send unsolicited mass messages. An email update every two weeks can keep interested visitors informed. Delay launching a Discord unless John has time to moderate it.

## Measurement and decisions

Track the path from a tagged post link to the game page, email signup, support-page visit, checkout start and successful payment. Use provider-confirmed payments to reconcile revenue; do not count a thank-you-page view as proof of payment. Avoid personal information in link tags. Respect applicable consent settings.

Weekly dashboard: qualified site visits by channel, email signups, returning visitors, support-page conversion, number of supporters, gross contributions, fees/refunds, net contributions, and hours spent on promotion. Where measurable, distinguish first-time from repeat supporters. Followers and video views are supporting indicators, not the objective on their own.

Illustration only: 1,000 qualified support-page visits × 1% completed contributions × $15 average = $150 gross. At 3%, the same arithmetic gives $450 gross. These assumptions are not industry benchmarks or predictions, and actual traffic/conversion are unknown.

If views do not produce visits, revise the hook/link and audience fit. If visitors do not understand the project, revise the landing page. If people start checkout but do not finish, inspect the payment experience. If support rises but promised work falls behind, narrow the milestone and report it. Begin with no paid advertising; consider a capped test only after organic traffic demonstrates that the page and payment flow work, and after John approves spending.
